A very tall, narrow vertical strip of transparent watercolour painting on white cold-pressed watercolour paper: the open ocean seen from inside it, from just under the surface at the very top down into the deep at the very bottom. Only water and light: it is the background for an animated film; the creatures will be painted over it later.
From top to bottom:
1) the top tenth: just under the surface, very light and bright: pale aqua and turquoise washes, lots of the white paper left showing as a dappled, rippling net of light, wet-in-wet.
2) the upper third: clear turquoise and cerulean, broad diagonal shafts of light lifted out of the wet paint, slanting down from the upper left and fading as they go; soft blooms where water dropped into damp paint; a few pale ghostly silhouettes of a distant kelp forest at the left and right edges, only lighter blue-green shapes.
3) the middle: cobalt and cerulean blue washes laid wet-in-wet, glazed again when dry, with hard-edged tide lines, cauliflower backruns, a sprinkle of salt texture (tiny pale starbursts) and visible granulation of ultramarine pigment; a little sap green and violet blended in places.
4) the lower third: deeper and darker: ultramarine into phthalo blue and indigo, with violet granulating in the paper's tooth; quieter, fewer marks; a few tiny pale specks of drifting marine snow.
5) the bottom: deep indigo-violet, still transparent, still showing the paper's texture.
The colour changes smoothly with depth; the painting is loose and luminous, by a master watercolourist. The left and right sides are similar so it can repeat side by side. Paint goes right to every edge. No fish, no whales, no animals, no plants in front, no seabed, no surface line, no people, no boats, no text, no signature, no border, no frame.
