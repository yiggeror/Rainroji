The attached picture is a rough layout of the left half of a long Chinese handscroll. Repaint it as a real masterpiece of Chinese ink-wash landscape painting (水墨山水, in the manner of the great Song and Yuan masters), keeping the composition exactly: every mountain, cliff, tree, waterfall and building stays where it is in the layout, at the same size and the same height, with the same outline against the sky, so the new painting lies precisely over the layout.
What is where, from left to right:
1) at the far left, a near cliff of dark rock rising from the water to about half the height, with an ancient twisted pine growing from its brow and reaching far out to the right over the water; on the cliff top at the left edge a tiny scholar sits with a qin.
2) open water below the pine: plain white paper, only a few faint horizontal ripple strokes; a very pale low line of far shore across it.
3) the main peak rising from mist at the bottom to the very top of the picture: built of stacked rounded rock masses (矾头) modelled with hemp-fibre texture strokes (披麻皴), pale tops and dark crevices, moss dots (点苔) along the ridges, rows of tiny distant trees on the ridges; a small temple among pines on its right shoulder.
4) on the right, a tall waterfall falling from the top of the picture in two leaps between two steep cliffs (axe-cut texture strokes, 斧劈皴), with small pines on the cliff brows; the falling water itself is untouched white paper between dark rock lips; at its foot a pool of white paper with a few dark wet stones.
5) pale, slender far peaks behind, in the palest ink washes, fading into mist.
Style: ink only, pure monochrome greys and blacks, wet brushwork with soft bleeding edges, dry flying-white strokes (飞白) on the rocks, layered from the palest washes to the darkest accents; generous empty space; bands of white mist across the middle of the mountain separating it into layers.
The paper is plain, flat, evenly lit warm off-white, with no texture, stains, borders, folds, mounting or vignette. No colour at all, no people other than the one scholar, no birds, no boats, no rain, no seals, no inscription, no calligraphy, no signature, no text.
