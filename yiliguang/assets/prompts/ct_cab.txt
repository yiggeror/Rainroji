One single object on its own, drawn in black Conté crayon on white laid paper in exactly the same technique as the reference drawing (Georges Seurat's tonal Conté crayon drawings): no outlines at all, only soft grainy tonal masses built up on the tooth of the paper, velvety blacks, greys made of tiny specks, edges that dissolve softly: a closed one-horse Paris cab (a fiacre) seen exactly from the side, facing LEFT, WITHOUT its horse: the boxy black coach body with a window, a small lit carriage lamp at each front corner glowing (paper white), the driver sitting high on the box at the front in a long cape and a top hat holding the reins and a whip, the two long shafts sticking forward to the left at the bottom, two large spoked wheels (the rear one larger), each wheel a clean circle with twelve thin spokes and a hub. The cab fills most of the picture's width; nothing else in the picture. Fully transparent background, no ground, no shadow, no horse, no text.
