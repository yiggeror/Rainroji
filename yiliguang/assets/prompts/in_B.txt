The attached picture is a rough layout of the right half of a long Chinese handscroll. Repaint it as a real masterpiece of Chinese ink-wash landscape painting (水墨山水, in the manner of the great Song and Yuan masters), keeping the composition exactly: every hill, cliff, tree, bridge and building stays where it is in the layout, at the same size and the same height, with the same outline, so the new painting lies precisely over the layout.
What is where, from left to right:
1) at the very left edge, the white falling water of a waterfall between dark cliffs (axe-cut texture strokes), the right cliff with small pines on its brow; at its foot a pool of white paper with dark wet stones.
2) close in front, a grove of tall bamboo in the rain: the nearest culms and leaves in the darkest ink, those behind paler; a few dark rocks at their feet.
3) a stream flowing out to the right, crossed by a single stone arch bridge (nobody on it), with a weeping willow at the near end of the bridge, its long fine branches hanging down.
4) behind, a range of low rounded hills in pale ink with tiny trees along their crests; at their foot along the river bank a quiet village: thatched cottages among trees of many kinds painted with different leaf-dots, low fences.
5) a small pavilion on stilts over the water at the right of the village.
6) on the right, the wide river: plain white paper with only a few faint ripple strokes; a clump of reeds in the near water; pale slender far peaks along the horizon fading into mist; the right edge is open water and empty mist.
Style: ink only, pure monochrome greys and blacks, wet brushwork with soft bleeding edges, dry flying-white strokes on rocks, layered from the palest washes to the darkest accents; generous empty space; white mist between the near and far things.
The paper is plain, flat, evenly lit warm off-white, with no texture, stains, borders, folds, mounting or vignette. No colour at all, no people, no birds, no boats, no rain, no seals, no inscription, no calligraphy, no signature, no text.
