A wide oil painting of a still pond at sunrise, painted in the plein-air technique of the French Impressionists: broken colour laid on in short, separate, visible brushstrokes of pure pigment side by side, thick paint, no blending, no outlines, luminous and soft. An original composition.
Layout (important): the far bank's foot, where the water begins, runs straight across the whole width at about 26% of the height from the top. Above it: the sky and the far bank. Below it, the whole lower three quarters: open water.
Sky: the sun is just rising behind mist, a soft, small, pale gold glow just above the far bank at about 46% of the width from the left; around it the sky is warm gold, going up into rose and peach, and at the very top a soft lilac blue; a few thin horizontal strands of rose-lit cloud.
Far bank: a long, low, irregular line of trees in the morning mist, soft blue-violet and mauve, varying in height, with rounded crowns; near the sun the mist and trees glow warm peach; toward the right edge the trees are taller and closer.
Water: perfectly calm, reflecting the sky upside down — gold and peach near the far bank, through rose and lilac to deeper cool blues and blue-violet at the bottom edge; the far trees' reflections hang down as soft broken violet verticals; from just below the sun a road of broken gold and white dabs runs straight down the water toward the viewer, widening as it comes; a band of pale mist lies on the far water; long, faint horizontal ripples.
Leave the water open and clear: no lily pads, no flowers, no boats, no bridge, no people, no willow branches, no foreground objects. No text, no signature, no frame.
