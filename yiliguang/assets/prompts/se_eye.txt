An extreme close-up of the side of a humpback whale's head around its eye, painted in loose, luminous transparent watercolour on white cold-pressed paper, for an animated film.
The eye sits at 60% of the picture's width from the left edge and exactly at mid-height, and it is small: about one fourteenth of the picture's width. It is an almond-shaped, dark, wet eye between heavy wrinkled lids, with a tiny white catchlight, looking out towards the left.
The whale's skin fills the whole picture, edge to edge: dark slate blue-grey and indigo over the upper half, laid wet-in-wet with soft blooms, backruns and granulating ultramarine; folds and creases of thick skin curving around the eye; a few small clusters of pale barnacles and old white scars scattered here and there, none near the eye; across the lower third, the skin turns paler towards the throat, with long parallel throat grooves running horizontally, white and pale blue-grey between darker lines. Light falls from above: the top of the head catches dappled light and a faint shimmer of caustics.
