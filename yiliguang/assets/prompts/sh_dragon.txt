Traditional Chinese shadow-puppet (piying) pieces, shown exactly as they look lit from behind against a glowing screen: cut from thin translucent dyed hide, the colours glowing like stained glass — vermilion red, emerald green, jade teal, amber yellow, with some black; covered in dense, delicate openwork carving (lattice, scrolling lines, rows of dots, whorls, scales, petal shapes) through which bright white light shows; thin dark brown cut edges. Perfectly flat and seen exactly from the side, no perspective, no shading, no shadows, no rods or sticks, no strings. Fully transparent background around every piece.
Three separate pieces of one jointed dragon puppet, in one row with wide empty gaps between them, all facing right and all the same height of body: (1) the dragon's head, facing right, jaws open, horns swept back, long whiskers, a flame-like mane, the neck ending in a straight cut on its left side; (2) one straight body segment: a horizontal length of scaly body of even thickness, a row of red spines along its top, a yellow belly band along its bottom, one small clawed leg below, straight cuts at both ends; (3) the tail segment: scaly body on the left ending in a large flame-like fan tail on the right. Green hide with red and amber details, carved scales throughout. No text, no border.
